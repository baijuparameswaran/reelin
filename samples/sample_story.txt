The Whistle at Lumen - Part 2

Seattle

Seven time zones west (from Antwerp Belgium), the sun was still up. At the Rusty Anchor in Seattle's
Ballard neighborhood, Dani was taking the stock of the drinks in the bar. This amount of 
preparation was not needed since the last Sounders final. She'd poured beer behind this bar for
eleven years and never once filled it for a World Cup match — not a real one,
not like this. Two days earlier the whole waterfront had still smelled like
gunpowder from the 250th, red-white-and-blue bunting not yet taken down from
the Independence Day crowds, and now half of it had been repurposed into USA
flags taped to the windows. "Round of 16," she told a regular, shaking her
head like she still didn't believe it. "Us. Round of 16."

Nobody in Seattle called the team favorites. Belgium had won more, spent more,
and carried names everyone recognized. But Dani had seen what the city could
do with a crowd behind it — she'd worked Sounders nights that shook the block
three streets over — and Lumen Field held sixty-eight thousand people who had
waited their whole lives for a night exactly like this one.
